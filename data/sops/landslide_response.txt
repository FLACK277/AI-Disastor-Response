LANDSLIDE EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROTOCOL
Standard Operating Procedure

1. IMMEDIATE ACTION (0-1 Hour)
- Treat active slope movement, falling rocks, and debris flow as life-threatening.
- Stop public access to the slide zone, downhill runout path, and blocked road segment.
- Move people uphill and away from drainage channels, retaining walls, and unstable slopes.
- Notify district administration, police, fire, public works, and medical response teams.
- Do not permit rescue entry until a safety officer confirms the slope is not actively failing.

2. SEARCH AND RESCUE
- Prioritize last-known locations of trapped persons, damaged homes, and vehicles.
- Use spotters to watch for additional rockfall, cracking, water seepage, or slope noise.
- Deploy ropes, helmets, lighting, stretchers, thermal imaging, and excavation tools.
- Maintain an entry log for rescue teams and rotate personnel to avoid fatigue.
- Suspend operations during heavy rain, aftershocks, or renewed slope movement.

3. EVACUATION AND TRAFFIC CONTROL
- Evacuate households below the slide and along likely debris-flow channels.
- Establish roadblocks upstream and downstream; divert traffic before the hazard area.
- Keep emergency lanes open for ambulances, earthmoving equipment, and utility crews.
- Move evacuees to pre-identified shelters away from steep slopes and riverbanks.

4. ENGINEERING AND UTILITIES
- Request geotechnical assessment before clearing debris or reopening roads.
- Check bridges, culverts, retaining walls, water lines, gas lines, and power poles.
- Drain pooled water only under engineering supervision to avoid sudden slope failure.
- Use excavators carefully; avoid undercutting the toe of the slope.

5. PUBLIC COMMUNICATION
- Issue warnings for additional landslides if rainfall continues.
- Tell residents to watch for cracks, tilting trees, jammed doors, muddy seepage, or rumbling sounds.
- Advise people not to cross debris fields, flooded streams, or damaged roads.
- Provide shelter location, helpline, and road closure updates at least every 2 hours.

6. RECOVERY
- Reopen roads and homes only after geotechnical and structural clearance.
- Document damaged property, casualties, missing persons, and resource needs.
- Plan slope stabilization, drainage repair, debris disposal, and long-term relocation if needed.
