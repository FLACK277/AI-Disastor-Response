CYCLONE AND SEVERE STORM RESPONSE PROTOCOL
Standard Operating Procedure

1. PRE-LANDFALL PREPAREDNESS
- Activate Emergency Operations Center when cyclone watch or warning is issued.
- Evacuate low-lying coastal communities, flood-prone areas, and unsafe housing.
- Open cyclone shelters with drinking water, food, sanitation, lighting, and medical support.
- Secure boats, ports, construction sites, loose signage, and power infrastructure.
- Pre-position NDRF teams, ambulances, generators, chainsaws, and communication equipment.

2. DURING LANDFALL
- Keep the public indoors and away from windows, coastlines, rivers, and fallen wires.
- Suspend field movement once wind speeds are unsafe except for life-saving operations.
- Track shelter occupancy, special-needs residents, and medical emergencies.
- Maintain backup communications through radio, satellite phones, or police networks.

3. STORM SURGE AND FLOODING
- Treat storm surge as a rapid evacuation hazard; do not wait for visible flooding.
- Move people vertically or inland according to local evacuation maps.
- Restrict travel through flooded roads, bridges, and underpasses.
- Deploy rescue boats only when conditions are safe for responders.

4. POST-LANDFALL RESPONSE
- Conduct rapid damage assessment for collapsed structures, blocked roads, and power hazards.
- Clear priority routes to hospitals, shelters, water plants, and relief warehouses.
- Restore power only after checking water contact, damaged poles, and exposed conductors.
- Begin water purification, vector control, debris clearance, and disease surveillance.

5. PUBLIC COMMUNICATION
- Provide updates on safe return, road status, water quality, relief distribution, and missing persons.
- Warn communities not to touch fallen wires, enter damaged buildings, or use contaminated water.
