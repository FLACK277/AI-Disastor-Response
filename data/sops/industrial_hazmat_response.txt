INDUSTRIAL ACCIDENT AND HAZMAT RESPONSE PROTOCOL
Standard Operating Procedure

1. SCENE SAFETY
- Approach from uphill, upwind, and upstream whenever possible.
- Establish hot, warm, and cold zones before rescue or medical operations.
- Identify the substance from labels, safety data sheets, plant operators, or emergency guides.
- Keep ignition sources away if flammable gas, vapor, or dust is suspected.
- Use appropriate PPE; do not enter the hot zone without trained hazmat personnel.

2. PUBLIC PROTECTION
- For toxic gas leaks, decide shelter-in-place or evacuation based on plume direction and concentration.
- Seal buildings, close windows, shut ventilation, and move people to upper floors for shelter-in-place.
- Evacuate crosswind first, then upwind, avoiding the plume path.
- Issue clear public messages naming the affected area, safe routes, and symptoms needing care.

3. MEDICAL AND DECONTAMINATION
- Set up decontamination before transporting exposed patients where practical.
- Remove contaminated clothing and rinse exposed skin with water unless substance guidance says otherwise.
- Track respiratory distress, burns, eye exposure, neurological symptoms, and delayed toxicity.
- Notify hospitals about the chemical, number of patients, and required isolation precautions.

4. CONTAINMENT AND CONTROL
- Shut valves, isolate power, plug leaks, dike drains, and stop runoff only if safe.
- Protect water bodies, storm drains, and food storage from contamination.
- Request specialist support from fire hazmat, pollution control board, industry experts, and police.

5. RECOVERY
- Maintain exclusion zones until monitoring confirms safe levels.
- Document exposure lists, cleanup actions, disposal chain, and environmental sampling.
- Provide public health follow-up for exposed communities and responders.
